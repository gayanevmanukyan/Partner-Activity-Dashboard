Data checks

Four things the file disagrees with itself about. The first is corrected here; the rest are reported as they are:

    The one structural point

    The dashboard mirrors the workbook: Active partners, Low activity and No activity are three tabs holding exactly the rows of the three sheets. Inside each one, the counters and the split show something the sheet cannot — what the monthly Bets columns say about those same rows.

    That is the whole point of splitting them this way. The Active partners tab shows 312 of its 325 rows still betting and 13 that are not; the No activity tab shows 514 confirmed and 4 that have taken bets this month. Each tab's list panel puts those disagreements at the top, so the sheets can be corrected row by row instead of re-checked wholesale.

    Projects that have never taken a single bet are counted as No activity, not as their own category — they just get their own counter and a filter, because “never started” and “stopped” need different conversations even when they sit in the same bucket.

    Once you trust the computed version, the three sheets can collapse into one with a single Bucket formula — or no bucket column at all, because the dashboard derives it.

    The three columns worth adding next

    These are what the placements tab is missing, in order of value:

    • Lobby category. Position 5 is meaningless without saying position 5 in what — Lobby Top, Popular, New Games. One column, and the whole tab gets a dimension.
    • Current position and Checked on. Right now the sheet records what was promised and nothing about what happened. With these two, the dashboard can show which deals are actually being honoured — that comparison is the reason to keep the sheet at all.
    • Partner ID / Project ID. Placements and offers both point at projects by name today. One rename and the links break silently.

    And for the offer round: the “October” header means next month needs a new column, which the dashboard cannot follow. A Month column with one row per project per round — the same shape as the placements sheet — keeps it readable and lets you compare rounds.
